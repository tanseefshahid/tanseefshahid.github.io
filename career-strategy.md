## Where You Are Right Now

- 6+ years production CV/ML across four companies (Ellexi → PERSPECTIVE → Luxolis → Hyvision)

- Current pay: 55M KRW (~$40K USD) + bonus + housing/food

## The Structural Truth

**You are not stuck because of your skills.** You are stuck because of citizenship and geography. No amount of additional prep, projects, or applications will fully fix a structural problem. You need structural moves.

Recent rejections (LMI, Ouster) were both visa-policy filters, not skill rejections. This is going to keep happening with smaller US/Canadian companies in 2026 unless you change strategy.

**Stop applying randomly. Pick a strategic path and commit.**

**Target list (apply to 3-5 per month):**

**Note:** Remote-from-Korea has 2-3x current pay ceiling but doesn't solve PR question.

### Track C: Profile-Builder Project (ONE thing, sustained)

The reason "no sponsorship" is the default answer is because at senior level there are more candidates than openings. To stand out enough to justify sponsorship cost, you need top-20% profile signals.

**Pick ONE of these. Commit 2-4 hours per week. Six-month timeline.**

- **Option 1 (highest signal):** Submit a paper to a workshop at a major conference (CVPR workshop, ICCV workshop, NeurIPS workshop). Build on your defect-segmentation custom loss work, or your laser-simulator hardware planning. Workshop bar is lower than main conference, but acceptance is real publication signal.
- **Option 2 (sustained signal):** Clean, well-documented GitHub project demonstrating modern technique relevant to your target jobs. Examples: implement ByteTrack from scratch, implement 3D Gaussian Splatting, build a complete mini-perception stack (detector + tracker + visualizer). Quality matters more than quantity — one excellent project beats five mediocre ones.
- **Option 3 (lower effort, broader signal):** Technical blog with 4-6 substantial posts demonstrating depth. Topics: your real custom loss work, the laser simulator, FoundationPose adaptation lessons, 3D point cloud algorithms in production C++.

**Pick by Friday of next week. Stop debating, start doing.**

### Track D: Stay in Current Job

**Do not quit Hyvision before having an offer.** Income matters. The job funds the search. Quitting early is the single biggest mistake in this situation.

Be a good employee. Ship the work. Don't burn the bridge. You may need a reference; you may stay longer than planned.

## Phase 3: Months 10-18 (Feb 2027 → Oct 2027) — If Still No NA Breakthrough

### Track G: Commit to Asian or European Hub for 2-3 Years

If NA continues to be closed, commit to Singapore, Tokyo, or Berlin as a stepping stone. Move there. Build PR there. Build network there. From those positions, NA becomes more accessible 3-5 years later.

**European options to consider:**

- **Netherlands:** Booking.com, ASML, TomTom, Adyen, Amsterdam startups. 30% tax ruling. PR in 5 years.
- **Germany:** SAP, Bosch (perception), Berlin startups. Blue Card with 21-month PR if basic German learned, 33 months otherwise.
- **Switzerland:** Google Zurich, Meta Zurich, ETH spinouts. Highest European compensation but visa harder for non-EU.

### Track H: The Korea Question

If still in Korea by 2027, decide explicitly: commit to learning Korean seriously (multi-year, 2+ hours/day) OR commit to leaving regardless of where you land. **The middle path of "hoping it gets better" without language investment doesn't work.**

## Realistic Compensation Step Ladder

You will not close a 5x salary gap in one move. Plan in steps:

| Step | Move | Compensation | Timeline |
|------|------|--------------|----------|
| Current | Hyvision, Korea | $40K | now |
| Step 1 | Remote from Korea OR Singapore/Tokyo move | $80-150K | 6-18 months |
| Step 2 | Canada via GTS OR established European hub | $130-180K | 18-36 months |
| Step 3 | US via H-1B OR senior role in Canada | $150-220K+ | 3-5 years |

**Climb, don't jump.** People who try to leap directly from $40K to $200K usually fail. People who climb in steps usually get there in 3-5 years.

## Hard Truths to Remember When Discouraged

1. **You are not behind.** You're in the middle of the structural-career-stretch that thousands of skilled non-Western engineers face. Your skills are real.

2. **You did not "play your education wrong."** Hindsight is brutal. Going to Korea on scholarship, joining startups for equity — these were reasonable decisions with the information you had at the time. The publication track is one path among many. Stop punishing yourself.

3. **The Ouster rejection was structural, not skill-based.** They didn't reject you because you weren't good enough. They rejected you because they wouldn't pay the cost to bring you in. That's a policy filter, not a verdict on you.

4. **Diffusion is the enemy.** Applying everywhere half-heartedly produces nothing. Commit to a strategic path for 6 months before reassessing. Track A is the path. Don't abandon it after one or two rejections.

5. **The profile-builder project is non-negotiable for top-tier outcomes.** Without something that makes you stand out from the pile, you're competing on the wrong axis. Six months of sustained side-project effort is the difference between $130K remote and $200K relocated.

6. **Stay in your current job.** Don't make the leap-of-faith mistake. Income funds the search. The current role is the bridge, not the prison.
